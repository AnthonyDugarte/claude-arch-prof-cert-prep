# Independent educational review

Reviewed 2026-09-24 against the user-supplied `claude-arch-professional-guide.md`, especially Sections 1–8. This is a content and assessment review, not a fact-check of every external product or legal source. I inspected `README.md`, `course/00-orientation.md`, all seven domain modules `course/01` through `course/07`, `course/08-capstone.md`, and `course/09-decision-templates.md`. At review time, the coverage map, consolidation file, and domains 1–3 research log referenced by the README were still being produced; I did not treat those expected indexing/provenance artifacts as missing teaching content.

## Verdict and coverage

The seven modules cover **all 38 detailed objectives** in the supplied blueprint: D1 6/6, D2 5/5, D3 8/8, D4 6/6, D5 5/5, D6 5/5, D7 3/3. Each objective has an identifiable teaching section or subsection. The material is generally at the intended professional level: it explains boundaries and alternatives, not just feature names. Strong examples include the D1 workflow/agent comparison and idempotency discussion, D2 cost per accepted outcome and context-loss repair, D3 permissioned retrieval and protocol selection, D4 first-failing-boundary diagnosis, D5 meaningful review and regulatory scoping, D6 decision records and handoff, and D7 settings/hook diagnosis. The capstone integrates all domains and explicitly distinguishes simulated from measured evidence. Orientation accurately treats guide facts as supplied-guide claims and avoids equating a 720 scaled score with 72% raw accuracy.

Each domain has six original questions, for **42 total**. Answers and rationales are present. I did not find a clear answer-key mismatch in the items inspected. The findings below concern lab completeness, release criteria, and how well some questions assess professional judgment.

## Must fix before calling the pack complete

1. **High — D4 lab promises fixtures that are absent.** In `course/04-evaluation-optimization.md`, “Practical lab — Diagnose a policy-refresh regression” begins “Given: a synthetic 60-case claims set ... two index snapshots ... request traces,” then requires learners to diagnose failures “from the supplied trace IDs.” The course folder contains no case set, snapshots, or traces. A learner cannot perform the stated reproducible diagnosis or meet its acceptance criterion. **Recommendation:** add a small actual synthetic fixture pack with labels, before/after retrieval IDs, representative traces and an answer key; or rewrite the lab as an explicit *design your own dataset and injected traces* exercise, replacing claims about supplied trace IDs with a concrete construction recipe and sample rows.

2. **High — Capstone scoring gate conflicts with its critical evidence requirements.** In `course/08-capstone.md`, “Acceptance rubric and oral defense” scores 1 as a “plausible explanation” and permits a score of 12/16 with no zero in identity, evaluation, or risk. The next paragraph requires deterministic demonstrations of forbidden actions and tenant isolation and a trace-backed diagnosis. Under the numeric gate, those critical areas can score 1 without tested evidence, yet the learner is described as passing the suggested gate. **Recommendation:** make the critical demonstrations explicit pass/fail prerequisites and require artifact-plus-test evidence (2/2) for identity/tool boundaries, evaluation quality, and risk/oversight; then apply the 12-point total only after those prerequisites pass. Include retrieval freshness if its revocation test is intended as a hard gate.

3. **Medium — Too many practice distractors test slogans rather than constrained architecture choices.** Across `course/01`–`course/07`, several items have one plausible answer and alternatives that no minimally qualified architect would choose: D1 question 1 contrasts a fixed workflow with an unbounded agent, five debating agents, and omitting validation; D3 question 3 contrasts lexical search with changing answer temperature, doubling output length, and deleting error codes; D5 question 5 offers “ask Claude to self-certify”; D7 question 5 contrasts outcome measures with prompt counts and one user's output volume. The guide's sample questions are illustrative, but these 42 questions alone give a learner little practice weighing two *credible* designs under latency, freshness, cost, authority, or review-capacity constraints. **Recommendation:** retain the simpler checks as warmups and add at least one harder case per domain with two defensible options under different constraints, a clear deciding fact, and rationales explaining when the rejected option would win. A small mixed-domain set would also test transfer between objectives.

## Optional improvements and item edits

1. **Medium — D4 question 3 states a stronger release rule than the lesson defines.** In `course/04-evaluation-optimization.md`, question 3 says unauthorized *tool attempts* doubled and keys B, “Block promotion under the security gate.” D4.1's example hard gate concerns unauthorized *write actions*, which an independent executor might have blocked; an attempted call is still a serious signal but does not automatically violate that stated gate. **Recommendation:** state an attempt-rate gate in the stem or lesson, or key the decision as “pause promotion and diagnose the attempts before deciding,” preserving the distinction between model behavior and executed side effects.

2. **Low — D2 question 2 does not establish its second control's relevance.** In `course/02-models-prompts-context.md`, question 2 describes unauthorized account information in valid JSON, then selects server-side authorization on *account tools* as one of two direct controls. The stem does not say that an account tool supplied the leaked data; the data might have entered via retrieval or an earlier context. **Recommendation:** identify the account-read tool in the scenario, or replace that option with a data-release/authorization check that applies regardless of source.

3. **Low — D1 lab introduces an undefined write path.** In `course/01-solution-design.md`, “Practical lab — Architect a policy-support assistant” specifies human-approved replies and asks learners to simulate “one timeout after an external write,” without naming that action. **Recommendation:** name an approved send/log action and its idempotency key, or mark the write timeout as a separate extension scenario. This will make the lab's reconciliation acceptance criterion testable.

4. **Low — Capstone lacks a completed exemplar for self-study calibration.** `course/08-capstone.md` includes a sound worked design defense and calculations but not a sample completed discovery brief, ADR, scored evaluation row, or release decision. The templates in `course/09-decision-templates.md` are empty apart from short examples. **Recommendation:** add one compact worked artifact or annotated exemplar showing how evidence earns 2 points under the rubric, including one failed target and resulting next experiment. This would calibrate learners without turning the capstone into a copy exercise.

## Scope limit

The review validates blueprint coverage and internal educational quality. It does not independently verify current Claude Code setting semantics, MCP/A2A specification details, model availability, certification eligibility, or the legal/regulatory claims cited in D5. Those volatile claims need the separate source validation and provenance pass before publication.

## Closure check — 2026-09-24

I re-inspected the revised D1, D2 and D4 labs/items, the capstone rubric and new exemplar, all 14 questions and defenses in `course/10-advanced-casebook.md`, and `reviews/resolution-record.md`. The D4 lab now explicitly has learners construct the 60 cases and paired traces, with required fields, representative failure rows and a reproducibility manifest; it no longer promises absent fixtures. The capstone requires 2/2 artifact-and-test evidence in four critical areas plus pass/fail authorization, revocation and trace-backed diagnosis demonstrations before the 12/16 score applies. Its simulated revocation artifact and failed-latency release example supply the requested calibration without claiming measured team results. These close the two high-severity findings.

The advanced casebook adds two cases per domain and asks for decisions across credible constraints. I checked A3's measured alternatives against its quality and p95 gates: only the keyed full-context choice satisfies both. A5, A7 and A13 now present meaningful competing paths with explanations of when they fit or fail. This is sufficient to close the practice-depth finding while retaining the 42 simpler module items as warmups; the casebook correctly says it is not a calibrated exam simulation. D4 question 3 now states an explicit attempt-rate gate, D2 question 2 names both leaking data paths, and the D1 lab names the write and reconciliation contract. Those item and lab findings are closed.

**Remaining must-fix findings from this review: none.** The earlier findings remain above as the review history. This closure checks the cited revisions, not every external-source claim or empirical course effectiveness.
